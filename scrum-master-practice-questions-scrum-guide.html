30 free Scrum Master practice questions on the 2020 Scrum Guide

Thirty questions, six from each part of the 2020 Scrum Guide, theory and values, the Scrum Team, the Sprint with Sprint Planning and Daily Scrum, Sprint Review and Retrospective, and the artifacts. Some ask for two answers.

The part that matters to me is the proof. After you check an answer, you get the sentence of the Scrum Guide that settles it. No opinions, no practices the guide does not prescribe, and if you think a question reads the guide wrongly, I would like to hear about it.

Question 1 · Theory and values

A Scrum Master asks why the Scrum Guide does not describe useful patterns and techniques that teams discover. What reason does the guide give?

Answer D. The guide limits itself to the definition of Scrum. Tactics depend on context, so they are left out and described elsewhere.

  • A. The guide says such patterns may be found, applied and devised within Scrum.
  • B. The guide gives no reason based on the age of a technique.
  • C. The guide mentions no such handbook. It says they are described elsewhere.
“Their description is beyond the purpose of the Scrum Guide because they are context sensitive and differ widely between Scrum uses.”
scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html

Question 2 · Theory and values

Instead of giving people detailed instructions, what does the Scrum Guide say the rules of Scrum do?

Answer C. Scrum avoids step-by-step instructions. Its rules shape how people work together, which leaves room for their own methods.

  • A. The guide says it does not give detailed instructions.
  • B. The guide does not describe job titles or reporting lines.
  • D. The guide does not name any tools.
“Rather than provide people with detailed instructions, the rules of Scrum guide their relationships and interactions.”
scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html

Question 3 · Theory and values

A Scrum Team stops producing a weekly status report that nobody reads. Which part of Scrum theory supports this choice?

Answer D. An unread report is waste. Lean thinking asks the team to drop it and focus on what matters.

  • A. Transparency is about making work visible, not keeping unread reports.
  • B. The value of commitment is about goals and mutual support, not documents.
  • C. Inspection depends on transparent artifacts, not on the number of reports.
“Lean thinking reduces waste and focuses on the essentials.”
scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html

Question 4 · Theory and values

With Scrum, important decisions are based on the perceived state of what?

Answer A. The Product Backlog, Sprint Backlog and Increment are the basis for decisions. That is why they must be transparent.

  • B. The guide bases decisions on the artifacts, not on seniority.
  • C. Performance reviews are not part of Scrum.
  • D. Velocity charts are not part of the 2020 Scrum Guide.
“With Scrum, important decisions are based on the perceived state of its three formal artifacts.”
scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html

Question 5 · Theory and values

In a company, Developers need a manager's approval for any change to how they work. What does the Scrum Guide say about this?

Answer B. Teams that must ask permission adapt slowly. The guide links quick adaptation to empowered, self-managing people.

  • A. The guide says a lack of empowerment makes adaptation harder.
  • C. The guide expects self-managing teams to adapt.
  • D. The guide gives the Product Owner no such role.
“Adaptation becomes more difficult when the people involved are not empowered or self-managing.”
scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html

Question 6 · Theory and values

A manager trusts the Developers to decide how to build a feature, treating them as capable and independent. Which value does this reflect?

Answer C. Respect in Scrum means seeing each other as capable, independent people. It also covers how others treat the team.

  • A. Courage is about doing the right thing, not how people regard each other.
  • B. Openness is about being open about the work and challenges.
  • D. Focus is about attention on the work of the Sprint.
“Scrum Team members respect each other to be capable, independent people, and are respected as such by the people with whom they work.”
scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html

Question 7 · The Scrum Team

A Scrum Team has grown to 16 people and decides to split into two Scrum Teams working on the same product. What should the two teams share?

Answer C. When a Scrum Team becomes too large, the guide suggests reorganizing into several cohesive Scrum Teams focused on the same product. Because they serve one product, they share one Product Goal, one Product Backlog and one Product Owner.

  • A. The guide says teams on the same product share the same Product Owner.
  • B. Teams on the same product share one Product Goal.
  • D. Each Scrum Team has its own Sprint Backlog and its own Daily Scrum for its Developers.
“If Scrum Teams become too large, they should consider reorganizing into multiple cohesive Scrum Teams, each focused on the same product. Therefore, they should share the same Product Goal, Product Backlog, and Product Owner.”
scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html

Question 8 · The Scrum Team

In one company, a separate operations group runs and maintains everything the Scrum Team builds. According to the guide, who is responsible for product-related activities such as verification, maintenance and operation?

Answer C. The guide makes the Scrum Team responsible for all product-related activities, from stakeholder collaboration to maintenance, operation and research. Handing these to another group splits work the guide keeps inside the team.

  • A. The guide places these activities with the Scrum Team, not an outside group.
  • B. The responsibility belongs to the whole Scrum Team, not one accountability.
  • D. The Scrum Guide does not describe a project management office.
“The Scrum Team is responsible for all product-related activities from stakeholder collaboration, verification, maintenance, operation, experimentation, research and development, and anything else that might be required.”
scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html

Question 9 · The Scrum Team

A team includes a tester, a designer and a data analyst along with two programmers. Who counts as a Developer in Scrum?

Answer D. The guide defines Developers by commitment to creating any part of a usable Increment, not by job title. The tester, designer and analyst are Developers just like the programmers.

  • A. The guide does not limit Developers to people who write code.
  • B. Job titles do not decide who is a Developer.
  • C. The guide does not describe temporary assignment by functional managers.
“Developers are the people in the Scrum Team that are committed to creating any aspect of a usable Increment each Sprint.”
scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html

Question 10 · The Scrum Team

Where does the Scrum Guide say the Product Owner's decisions become visible?

Answer A. The guide points to two places, the content and order of the Product Backlog and the Increment inspected at the Sprint Review. That is where the organization can see and respect the Product Owner's choices.

  • B. The Sprint Retrospective is about quality and effectiveness, not Product Owner decisions.
  • C. The Product Owner does not assign tasks to Developers.
  • D. The guide does not describe an impediment list.
“These decisions are visible in the content and ordering of the Product Backlog, and through the inspectable Increment at the Sprint Review.”
scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html

Question 11 · The Scrum Team

Which statement matches what the 2020 Scrum Guide says about the Scrum Team's effectiveness?

Answer A. The guide makes the Scrum Master accountable for the Scrum Team's effectiveness. The Scrum Master does this by enabling the team to improve its practices within Scrum. Velocity is not part of the guide.

  • B. The guide gives this accountability to the Scrum Master, not to functional managers.
  • C. The guide names the Scrum Master as accountable.
  • D. The guide does not prescribe velocity or any such measure.
“The Scrum Master is accountable for the Scrum Team’s effectiveness. They do this by enabling the Scrum Team to improve its practices, within the Scrum framework.”
scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html

Question 12 · The Scrum Team

Which two statements about the Product Owner are true according to the guide? (Select TWO.)

Answer B, C. One Product Owner can represent many stakeholders in the Product Backlog. Making sure the Product Backlog is transparent, visible and understood is part of their Product Backlog management.

  • A. The Product Owner remains accountable after delegating.
  • D. How work is turned into Increments is at the sole discretion of the Developers.
  • E. The entire organization must respect the Product Owner's decisions.
“The Product Owner may represent the needs of many stakeholders in the Product Backlog. [...] Ensuring that the Product Backlog is transparent, visible and understood.”
scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html

Question 13 · The Sprint, Sprint Planning and Daily Scrum

A department head wants to add three weekly coordination meetings for a Scrum Team. What does the guide say the Scrum events are meant to do in this respect?

Answer D. The guide says the events create regularity and reduce the need for meetings that Scrum does not define. Adding many extra meetings suggests the events are not doing their job.

  • A. The guide aims to minimize extra meetings, not to pile events on top of them.
  • B. The Product Owner is not given the role of choosing which meetings to replace.
  • C. The events keep their prescribed cadence whatever other meetings exist.
“Events are used in Scrum to create regularity and to minimize the need for meetings not defined in Scrum.”
scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html

Question 14 · The Sprint, Sprint Planning and Daily Scrum

A sponsor asks how Scrum makes delivery more predictable. Which answer reflects the Scrum Guide?

Answer C. The guide links predictability to the Sprint. Each Sprint ensures progress toward the Product Goal is inspected and adapted at least every calendar month.

  • A. Fixing all scope upfront is not how the guide describes predictability.
  • B. The guide caps Sprints at one month and does not require two weeks.
  • D. Weekly reports by the Scrum Master are not part of the guide.
“Sprints enable predictability by ensuring inspection and adaptation of progress toward a Product Goal at least every calendar month.”
scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html

Question 15 · The Sprint, Sprint Planning and Daily Scrum

At the start of Sprint Planning, which part of the first topic is the Product Owner's to propose?

Answer A. For the first topic, the guide has the Product Owner propose how the product could increase its value and utility in the Sprint. The whole Scrum Team then shapes the Sprint Goal from there.

  • B. Developers are self-managing and decide who does what.
  • C. Planning the work is for the Developers, and the guide does not require hour estimates.
  • D. The Definition of Done is not set by the Product Owner during the first topic.
“The Product Owner proposes how the product could increase its value and utility in the current Sprint.”
scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html

Question 16 · The Sprint, Sprint Planning and Daily Scrum

A team using one-month Sprints asks for the Sprint Planning timebox. What is it?

Answer D. Sprint Planning is timeboxed to a maximum of eight hours for a one-month Sprint. Four hours is the Sprint Review timebox for the same Sprint length.

  • A. Two hours is not a timebox the guide gives for Sprint Planning.
  • B. Four hours is the maximum for the Sprint Review of a one-month Sprint.
  • C. Twelve hours exceeds the maximum the guide sets.
“Sprint Planning is timeboxed to a maximum of eight hours for a one-month Sprint.”
scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html

Question 17 · The Sprint, Sprint Planning and Daily Scrum

A Developer says changes to the plan may only be made during the Daily Scrum. What does the guide say?

Answer B. The guide says the Daily Scrum is not the only time Developers may adjust their plan. They often meet during the day to discuss adapting or re-planning the rest of the Sprint's work.

  • A. The guide explicitly says the Daily Scrum is not the only moment.
  • C. The Sprint Backlog keeps changing after Sprint Planning as more is learned.
  • D. The Developers own their plan and need no approval to change it.
“The Daily Scrum is not the only time Developers are allowed to adjust their plan.”
scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html

Question 18 · The Sprint, Sprint Planning and Daily Scrum

A team wants to drop the classic three questions at the Daily Scrum. Which two conditions does the guide set for whatever format they choose? (Select TWO.)

Answer A, D. The guide lets Developers use any structure and techniques. The only conditions are that the Daily Scrum focuses on progress toward the Sprint Goal and produces an actionable plan for the next day.

  • B. The guide does not prescribe three questions.
  • C. Recording answers is not required by the guide.
  • E. The Developers choose the format without needing approval.
“The Developers can select whatever structure and techniques they want, as long as their Daily Scrum focuses on progress toward the Sprint Goal and produces an actionable plan for the next day of work.”
scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html

Question 19 · Sprint Review and Sprint Retrospective

A team works in two-week Sprints. How does the Scrum Guide describe the length of its Sprint Review compared with a one-month Sprint?

Answer A. The guide gives the maximum for a one-month Sprint and says that for shorter Sprints the event is usually shorter. It does not give an exact formula.

  • B. The guide does not prescribe a proportional calculation.
  • C. Four hours is a maximum for one-month Sprints, not a fixed length for all Sprints.
  • D. Stakeholders do not set the timebox of Scrum events.
“For shorter Sprints, the event is usually shorter.”
scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html

Question 20 · Sprint Review and Sprint Retrospective

A Scrum Master notices that Sprint Reviews have become one-way slide shows with no questions. What does the Scrum Guide say about this?

Answer D. The guide calls the Sprint Review a working session. A one-way presentation misses the collaboration the event is meant to produce.

  • A. The guide warns against limiting the event to a presentation.
  • B. The guide does not describe the Sprint Review as an acceptance meeting.
  • C. Collaboration happens during the event, not only afterwards.
“The Sprint Review is a working session and the Scrum Team should avoid limiting it to a presentation.”
scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html

Question 21 · Sprint Review and Sprint Retrospective

During a Sprint Retrospective, the team looks at its tools, its processes and how its members interact. Which other item does the Scrum Guide explicitly list for inspection in this event?

Answer C. The guide lists individuals, interactions, processes, tools and the Definition of Done. The Retrospective is therefore a natural place to strengthen the Definition of Done.

  • A. Progress toward the Product Goal is discussed at the Sprint Review. It is not listed for the Retrospective.
  • B. Pay is not a Scrum topic at all.
  • D. The guide does not mention satisfaction scores.
“The Scrum Team inspects how the last Sprint went with regards to individuals, interactions, processes, tools, and their Definition of Done.”
scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html

Question 22 · Sprint Review and Sprint Retrospective

A manager suggests holding the Sprint Review and Sprint Retrospective after the Sprint ends, in a separate review week. What does the Scrum Guide say?

Answer C. The guide says all the work needed to achieve the Product Goal, including the Sprint Review and Sprint Retrospective, happens within Sprints. A review week outside the Sprint is not part of Scrum.

  • A. Team agreement does not change where the events happen.
  • B. The Sprint Retrospective concludes the Sprint, so it is inside it.
  • D. The guide describes no review week.
“All the work necessary to achieve the Product Goal, including Sprint Planning, Daily Scrums, Sprint Review, and Sprint Retrospective, happen within Sprints.”
scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html

Question 23 · Sprint Review and Sprint Retrospective

During refinement, a Product Owner thinks the Developers' size for an item is too large. What role does the Scrum Guide give the Product Owner here?

Answer B. Developers own the sizing. The Product Owner can still help them understand and choose trade-offs, which may lead them to a different size.

  • A. Sizing is the responsibility of the Developers.
  • C. The guide sets no escalation path for sizing.
  • D. The Product Owner takes part and may influence the Developers.
“The Product Owner may influence the Developers by helping them understand and select trade-offs.”
scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html

Question 24 · Sprint Review and Sprint Retrospective

Which statements about Product Backlog refinement match the Scrum Guide? (Select TWO.)

Answer A, C. Refinement is ongoing and adds details such as description, order and size. The Developers who will do the work size the items.

  • B. Refinement is not a Scrum event and has no timebox.
  • D. Sizing belongs to the Developers who will do the work.
  • E. The Product Backlog is refined as needed throughout the Sprint.
“This is an ongoing activity to add details, such as a description, order, and size.”
scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html

Question 25 · Artifacts and commitments

Why does the Scrum Guide attach a commitment to each artifact?

Answer C. The guide says the commitments reinforce empiricism and the Scrum values. They give a clear target against which progress can be measured and adapted.

  • A. Commitments are not contracts. They exist to support empiricism and the values.
  • B. Commitments support inspection, they do not replace it.
  • D. The Product Backlog is emergent, so commitments do not freeze scope.
“These commitments exist to reinforce empiricism and the Scrum values for the Scrum Team and their stakeholders.”
scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html

Question 26 · Artifacts and commitments

A Product Owner thinks the Developers sized an item far too large. What does the Scrum Guide allow the Product Owner to do?

Answer A. The Product Owner may influence sizing, but only by helping the Developers see trade-offs. The size itself remains with the Developers.

  • B. Sizing is the Developers' responsibility, so the Product Owner cannot overwrite it.
  • C. The Scrum Master has no sizing authority.
  • D. Removing items to force a size is not what the guide describes.
“The Product Owner may influence the Developers by helping them understand and select trade-offs.”
scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html

Question 27 · Artifacts and commitments

A department manager adds tasks directly to the team's Sprint Backlog without talking to anyone. Whose plan is the Sprint Backlog according to the Scrum Guide?

Answer A. The Developers create and own the Sprint Backlog. A manager adding tasks bypasses the people who own the plan and weakens self-management.

  • B. The Product Owner is accountable for the Product Backlog, not the Sprint Backlog plan.
  • C. Stakeholders do not edit the Developers' plan.
  • D. The Scrum Master does not own the Sprint Backlog.
“The Sprint Backlog is a plan by and for the Developers.”
scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html

Question 28 · Artifacts and commitments

Midway through a Sprint, the Developers learn that the selected work is larger than expected. What should they do?

Answer C. Scope in the Sprint Backlog can flex, the Sprint Goal does not. The Developers work it out with the Product Owner.

  • A. The guide says scope is negotiated without affecting the Sprint Goal.
  • B. Sprints have a fixed length and are not extended.
  • D. Quality does not decrease, and the Definition of Done stays in force.
“If the work turns out to be different than they expected, they collaborate with the Product Owner to negotiate the scope of the Sprint Backlog within the Sprint without affecting the Sprint Goal.”
scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html

Question 29 · Artifacts and commitments

At what moment does the Scrum Guide say an Increment is born?

Answer A. An Increment exists as soon as an item meets the Definition of Done. Timing within the Sprint does not matter.

  • B. The Sprint Review presents Increments that already exist.
  • C. Release comes after an Increment exists, not before.
  • D. Increments can be born at any point in the Sprint.
“The moment a Product Backlog item meets the Definition of Done, an Increment is born.”
scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html

Question 30 · Artifacts and commitments

Under deadline pressure, a Developer suggests skipping a review step that the Definition of Done requires, just for this Sprint. What does the Scrum Guide say?

Answer B. Conforming to the Definition of Done is not optional. Skipping a step means the work is not Done and cannot be part of an Increment.

  • A. The guide makes no exception for Product Owner agreement.
  • C. The Scrum Master has no power to waive the Definition of Done.
  • D. The guide does not describe hardening Sprints.
“The Developers are required to conform to the Definition of Done.”
