What does SELECT name, points, RANK() OVER (ORDER BY points DESC) AS rnk, ... return? SQL Window Functions

Block 6, Window Functions, question 9 of the SQL sample

CREATE TABLE contest2 (id INTEGER, name TEXT, points INTEGER);
INSERT INTO contest2 VALUES
  (1, 'Fay', 70),
  (2, 'Gus', 70),
  (3, 'Hana', 65),
  (4, 'Ivo', 60);


SELECT name, points,
       RANK() OVER (ORDER BY points DESC) AS rnk,
       DENSE_RANK() OVER (ORDER BY points DESC) AS drnk
FROM contest2
ORDER BY points DESC, name

What does this query return?

  1. A. Fay | 70 | 1 | 1 Gus | 70 | 1 | 1 Hana | 65 | 3 | 2 Ivo | 60 | 4 | 3
  2. B. Fay | 70 | 1 | 1 Gus | 70 | 1 | 1 Hana | 65 | 2 | 3 Ivo | 60 | 3 | 4
  3. C. Fay | 70 | 1 | 1 Gus | 70 | 2 | 2 Hana | 65 | 3 | 3 Ivo | 60 | 4 | 4
  4. D. Fay | 70 | 1 | 1 Gus | 70 | 1 | 1 Hana | 65 | 2 | 2 Ivo | 60 | 3 | 3
Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong

Answer, A. Fay | 70 | 1 | 1 Gus | 70 | 1 | 1 Hana | 65 | 3 | 2 Ivo | 60 | 4 | 3

A. Fay | 70 | 1 | 1 Gus | 70 | 1 | 1 Hana | 65 | 3 | 2 Ivo | 60 | 4 | 3
Correct. Both functions tie Fay and Gus at 1, then RANK jumps to 3 for Hana because two rows sit above it, while DENSE_RANK moves to 2 with no gap.
B. Fay | 70 | 1 | 1 Gus | 70 | 1 | 1 Hana | 65 | 2 | 3 Ivo | 60 | 3 | 4
This swaps the two columns for the non tied rows, giving RANK the gapless numbers that belong to DENSE_RANK and handing DENSE_RANK the skipped numbers that belong to RANK.
C. Fay | 70 | 1 | 1 Gus | 70 | 2 | 2 Hana | 65 | 3 | 3 Ivo | 60 | 4 | 4
This applies ROW_NUMBER logic to both columns, breaking the tie between Fay and Gus instead of letting RANK and DENSE_RANK agree on 1 for both of them.
D. Fay | 70 | 1 | 1 Gus | 70 | 1 | 1 Hana | 65 | 2 | 2 Ivo | 60 | 3 | 3
This applies DENSE_RANK logic to both columns, so RANK never skips ahead after the tie even though it counts rows above the current one rather than distinct values.

RANK and DENSE_RANK only disagree once a tie has happened, and only about what comes right after it. RANK counts rows above the current one, so a tie of size two is followed by a jump of two, while DENSE_RANK counts distinct values and always moves to the next integer.
