What does print(a[np.newaxis, :].shape) print? NumPy Broadcasting and Shapes

Block 3, Broadcasting and Shapes, question 8 of the NumPy sample

import numpy as np
a = np.arange(3)
print(a[np.newaxis, :].shape)

What does this print?

  1. A. (3, 1)
  2. B. (1, 1, 3)
  3. C. (1, 3)
  4. D. (3,)
Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong

Answer, C. (1, 3)

A. (3, 1)
Places the new axis after the existing one instead of before it.
B. (1, 1, 3)
Assumes np.newaxis inserts two new axes instead of exactly one.
C. (1, 3)
Correct. np.newaxis inserts a size 1 axis exactly where it appears in the index, before the existing axis, turning shape (3,) into (1, 3).
D. (3,)
Assumes indexing with np.newaxis alongside a full slice leaves the shape completely unchanged.

np.newaxis inserts exactly one size 1 axis at the position it occupies in the index tuple, everything else about the array stays the same. Putting it before the colon makes a row shape (1, 3), putting it after would make a column shape (3, 1) instead.

The answer above is what NumPy 2.4.4 printed when the snippet was run, not what someone expected it to do.
